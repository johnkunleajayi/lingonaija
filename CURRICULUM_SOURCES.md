# Five-lesson curriculum sources

Reviewed 9 October 2026. Each language has Getting Started (three lessons) and Everyday Life (two lessons), with exactly four contextual exercises per lesson. Existing published welcome lessons and Yorùbá Everyday Greetings are preserved. New scenario prompts and explanations are original; language forms and meanings were checked against the references below. Names in productive name-introduction patterns are example names, not new translations.

## Yorùbá

- `yoruba-phrases`: [Omniglot: useful Yorùbá phrases](https://www.omniglot.com/language/phrases/yoruba.php). Introduce Yourself: Orúkọ mi ni … and respectful/plural Kí ni orúkọ yín?.
- `yoruba-ch5`: [University of Texas COERLL, Yorùbá Yé Mi, chapter 5](https://coerll.utexas.edu/yemi/pdfs/yy_ch5.pdf). Introduce Yourself: Mo jẹ́ akẹ́kọ̀ọ́ (student), printed p.115. Family & People: Èmi ni màmáa Túndé, Èmi ni bàbáa Kóyè and Olú ni àbúrò Adé, p.116; Ta ni bàbáà rẹ?, p.119. Àbúrò means younger sibling and does not alone specify gender. The textbook's contracted family forms are retained.
- `yoruba-ch7`: [Yorùbá Yé Mi, chapter 7](https://coerll.utexas.edu/yemi/pdfs/yy_ch7.pdf). Food & Drink: Ẹ kú ìjẹun o! at a meal (p.168); Mo fẹ́ mu omi, Mo fẹ́ jẹ ìrẹsì and Mo lè mu omi (p.169). The older PDF font has unreliable text extraction; display uses Unicode Yorùbá characters.

## Igbo

- `igbo-nkenne`: [NKENNE, Basic Igbo Greetings and Introductions](https://www.nkenne.com/blog/basic-igbo-greetings-and-introductions). Everyday Greetings: Ụtụtụ ọma, Ehihie ọma and Mgbede ọma. These are common modern teaching forms; greetings vary by community and situation.
- `igbo-phrases`: [Omniglot: useful Igbo phrases](https://www.omniglot.com/language/phrases/igbo.php). Everyday Greetings: Ka chi foo, a night-time farewell, not a substitute for every evening greeting. Food & Drink: Rie nke ọma as a meal wish.
- `igbo-introductions`: [Harvard ELIAS, introducing self and others](https://elias.fas.harvard.edu/node/274). Introduce Yourself: Aha m bụ Ada, Kedụ aha gị?, M bụ nwa akwụkwọ, M na-asụ Igbo. Family & People: Aha nna m bụ Obi and Aha enyi m bụ Ada. Source typography varies; vowel underdots and bụ are displayed consistently with the source's other examples.
- `igbo-family`: [Harvard ELIAS, Igbo family system](https://elias.fas.harvard.edu/node/269). Family & People: Nke a bụ nne m and Nne m na nna m.
- `igbo-food`: [Harvard ELIAS, Food](https://elias.fas.harvard.edu/languages/igbo/beginning/19/food). Food & Drink: Agụụ na-agụ m, Mmiri na-agụ m and M na-esi nri. Source hyphen spacing is normalised, without changing meaning.

## Hausa

- `hausa-greetings`: [University of Wisconsin, Hausa Greetings](https://wisc.pb.unizin.org/lctlresources/chapter/hausa-greetings/). Everyday Greetings: Ina kwana? and Sai an jima. Family & People: Ina iyali?, Ina yara?, Ina miji?, Ina mata?, with Lafiya lau as a positive reply. These are well-being questions in greeting context; they are not taught as literal location questions. Examples involving spouses explicitly establish the relationship rather than assume it.
- `hausa-rti-greetings`: [RTI Press, What Works in Early Grade Literacy Instruction, archived by ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED594144.pdf), Figure 12 (printed p.18) and Annex 1 (printed p.38), Nigerian Hausa lesson schedule and Waƙar Gaisuwa. The greeting example pairs Ina kwana with safe (morning), and Ina wuni with rana (daytime). Everyday Greetings uses Ina wuni? in an afternoon situation; it asks about the day and can also occur later in the day. Only the short language form is referenced; the song and teaching schedule are not reproduced.
- `hausa-phrases`: [Omniglot: useful Hausa phrases](https://www.omniglot.com/language/phrases/hausa.php). Everyday Greetings: Barka da yamma. Introduce Yourself: Sunana …, Mi sunanka? (male addressee), Mi sunanki? (female addressee), Na fito daga … (origin, with Kano as the example place). The source's afternoon categorisation is not used.
- `hausa-food`: [University of Wisconsin, Basic Expressions about Food](https://wisc.pb.unizin.org/lctlresources/chapter/basic-expressions-about-food/). Food & Drink: Ina jin yunwa, Ina son abarba, Ba na son kaza. Son can mean like/want; the fruit-choice situation establishes want, while the chicken example states a preference, not an allergy.
- `hausa-peace-corps`: [Peace Corps Hausa language lessons, archived by ERIC](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED290338.pdf). Introduce Yourself: name exchanges; Food & Drink: ruwa (water). Modern standard written spelling is used rather than phonetic transcription. Hausa boko writing normally omits tone marks; no invented tone notation is added.

## Scope and maintenance

The new JSON lesson entries list their `sourceIds`. Wrong options reuse sourced forms from this curriculum or English situational interpretations; they are deliberately wrong for the stated context, not purported alternative translations. Preserve Igbo underdots and Yorùbá tone marks in display. These concise lessons introduce practical exchanges, not exhaustive grammar or claims of universal dialect usage. Conversation Practice content and evaluation are unchanged; its sources remain in the existing documentation. No external photographs or other assets were added.
